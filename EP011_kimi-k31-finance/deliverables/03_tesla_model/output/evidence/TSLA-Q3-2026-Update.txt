In Q3, we maintained strong momentum on many projects spanning Super Intelligence (SI), 
robotics and renewable energy that will be critical to building a future of Amazing 
Abundance. Strength in our core business also continued, and we achieved our second 
consecutive quarter of TTM revenue in excess of $100B. 
FSD (Supervised)⁴ attach rate continued to grow, reaching over 75% of new vehicle 
deliveries in North America –  our most mature market –  a nearly twenty percentage point 
sequential increase. We believe that FSD is driving demand in markets where it is available, 
and we continue to work with regulators to get it approved in other markets. 
We started providing Robotaxi rides using Cybercabs on September 3 in Austin. Cumulative 
unsupervised Robotaxi miles grew eightfold quarter over quarter, driven by sustained 
double-digit weekly growth.
We ended the quarter with the largest backlog for our passenger vehicles in four years. We 
rolled out the updated Tesla Semi that is produced on our high -volume manufacturing line in 
Nevada and unveiled the Next Gen Roadster on October 15. While battery availability 
continued to be the limiting factor in increasing auto volumes in the quarter, we have made 
additional progress in securing future supply, as we work toward our goal of maximum 
utilization of our factories. 
Demand for our Energy Storage business remained strong in the quarter, with our highest -
ever order rate and backlog. Energy storage is the fastest way to add power to the existing 
grid and is critical to data center uptime. We expect rising data center demand to continue 
driving demand for our products. Megafactory Texas began production, with planned 
capacity of 50 GWh. We also advanced LFP supply domestication in the U.S., shipping the 
first Megapacks built with LFP cells from our factory in Nevada.
We continue to invest in the future through R&D and Capex spend across our businesses, 
including SI, robotics and energy. Much hard work lies ahead, but we are as optimistic as 
ever. Thank you to our supporters as we continue to execute on many challenging and 
important projects.

Automotive
We achieved record quarterly deliveries in several markets, including Canada, Australia, 
Japan and Taiwan. Given the strong demand we are seeing across various markets, we 
remain focused on increasing production across our factories and attained record 
production at Gigafactory Berlin. 
We began volume production of Tesla Semi, our Class 8 heavy- duty truck. The top variant 
can go 500 miles at full 82,000 lbs gross combination weight while achieving an efficiency 
of 1.7 kWh/mile. That efficiency allows the Tesla Semi to use a third of the energy used by a 
comparable diesel truck to haul the same payload. In a transportation market where energy 
is one of the largest components of total cost per mile, this will provide a meaningful 
advantage to Tesla Semi operators.
Energy Generation and Storage
We began production at Megafactory Texas. Energy storage demand is increasingly aligned 
with SI infrastructure demand as grid constraints and power availability create more 
opportunities for Megapack to be deployed. Megapack excels in off- grid and on-grid 
applications, supplying large data center loads, buffering oscillating demand, helping 
interconnection grid-compliance and strengthening the grid –  all with less local fossil fuel 
generation.
Robotics
Optimus production began in Fremont on our high- volume lines. The bots coming off this 
line are the first production- quality bots that we have manufactured. While we will continue 
to iterate on the hardware and manufacturing process, we have proven that the current 
manufacturing process for Optimus is suitable for a commercial product.

SI Compute
We plan to reach 400 MW of SI training compute by the end of the year, supporting the 
development of both vehicle and humanoid robot autonomy software. Additionally, we 
continued construction for the Austin Fab, where we plan to start commissioning equipment 
in 2027.
Battery
We continue to make progress expanding battery manufacturing capacity and securing 
future supply, even as battery availability remains a limiting factor on the pace of our global 
vehicle production ramp. Critical projects include vehicle pack capacity in Berlin, cathode 
material production and lithium refining in Texas and LFP cells in Nevada for our energy 
storage products. We shipped the first Megapacks comprised solely of our own LFP cells, an 
important milestone to localizing this important battery type. We continue to increase 
production of our 4680 cells to support the ramps of both Cybercab and Tesla Semi along 
with higher Model Y volumes.
Other Supporting Infrastructure
We added over 2,100 net new Supercharging stalls, growing the network by 14% year -over-
year. Our Supercharger for Business program also grew to over 600 stalls. These customer -
owned chargers allow us to further scale the charging network while ensuring the same 
performance standards and service standard as Tesla -owned locations. We deployed our 
first Accordion Supercharger, which is pre- assembled in the factory and cheaper to install. 
We also deployed the first pre -assembled Megacharger to support the Tesla Semi ramp.

FSD adoption continued to increase during the quarter, including record net new 
subscriptions. We also achieved record FSD attach rates in North America, with over 75% of

than 160 million kilometers since initial approval.
Robotaxi
We began deployment of Cybercab into our Robotaxi fleet in Austin. We reached nearly two 
million cumulative unsupervised miles for our Robotaxi service as of the end of the quarter. 
Operating hours in Austin are now 6am to midnight, and we expect to reduce wait times via 
increased fleet supply and improvement of Robotaxi orchestration algorithms. Grok is available 
in Cybercab with access to Grok Bot and Connectors, which allows users to manage their 
inbox, calendars, files, chats and tasks –  all hands free via voice command –  
making Cybercab 
a mode of transport, a productivity space and, with our entertainment options, a second living 
room.
Automotive Services
Services and Other gross profit grew $86M sequentially to a record $734M, achieving a 
15.9% gross margin, with growth driven by charging, insurance and part sales. We continue to 
prudently manage these supporting business segments to ensure we can improve efficiency 
without sacrificing customer experience.

CERTAIN TERMS
When used in this update, certain terms have the following meanings. Our vehicle deliveries include only vehicles that have been transferred to end customers with all paperwork correctly completed. Our energy product deployment volume 
includes both customer units when installed and equipment sales at time of delivery. “Net income attributable to common stockholders (non-GAAP)" is equal to (i) net income attributable to common stockholders before (ii)(a) stock-based 
compensation expense, net of tax, (b) digital assets unrealized (gain) loss, net of tax, (c) SpaceX equity investment unrealized (gain) loss, net of tax and (d) certain tax items. "Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP)" is equal to (i) net income attributable 
to common stockholders before (ii)(a) interest expense, (b) provision for (benefit from) income taxes, (c) depreciation, amortization and impairment, (d) stock-based compensation expense (e) digital assets unrealized (gain) loss and (f) SpaceX 
equity investment unrealized (gain) loss. "Free cash flow" is operating cash flow less capital expenditures. Average cost per vehicle is cost of automotive sales divided by new vehicle deliveries (excluding operating leases). “Days sales 
outstanding” is equal to (i) average accounts receivable, net for the period divided by (ii) total revenues and multiplied by (iii) the number of days in the period. “Days payable outstanding” is equal to (i) average accounts payable for the period 
divided by (ii) total cost of revenues and multiplied by (iii) the number of days in the period. “Days of supply” is calculated by dividing new car ending inventory by the relevant period's deliveries and using trading days. Constant currency 
impacts are calculated by comparing actuals against current results converted into USD using average exchange rates from the prior period.
NON-GAAP FINANCIAL INFORMATION
Consolidated financial information has been presented in accordance with GAAP as well as on a non-GAAP basis to supplement our consolidated financial results. Our non-GAAP financial measures include non-GAAP net income (loss) 
attributable to common stockholders, non-GAAP net income (loss) attributable to common stockholders on a diluted per share basis (calculated using weighted average shares for GAAP diluted net income (loss) attributable to common 
stockholders), Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA margin, non-GAAP automotive gross margin and free cash flow. These non-GAAP financial measures also facilitate management’s internal comparisons to Tesla’s historical performance as 
well as comparisons to the operating results of other companies. Management believes that it is useful to supplement its GAAP financial statements with this non-GAAP information because management uses such information internally for its 
operating, budgeting and financial planning purposes. Management also believes that presentation of the non-GAAP financial measures provides useful information to our investors regarding our financial condition and results of operations, so 
that investors can see through the eyes of Tesla management regarding important financial metrics that Tesla uses to run the business and allowing investors to better understand Tesla’s performance. Non-GAAP information is not prepared 
under a comprehensive set of accounting rules and therefore, should only be read in conjunction with financial information reported under U.S. GAAP when understanding Tesla’s operating performance. A reconciliation between GAAP and 
non-GAAP financial information is provided above.
